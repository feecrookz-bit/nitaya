# Royale Stones, studied

royalestones.co.uk is the main competitor. This is what their site does, read from the live pages on 14 September 2026 (home, product, category, delivery, trade, support, showrooms, cart), what the demo has taken from it, and what it has deliberately left alone. Prices quoted are theirs on that day; they will move.

## Who they are

- A tile and paving retailer with five showrooms (Lincoln, Watford, Peterborough, Birmingham, Kent) and two warehouses. Watford is the one that matters: it is fifteen miles from Mark Road.
- Far wider range: outdoor porcelain in six sizes and three tiers, natural stone, indoor tiles, bathrooms, pergolas, decking, adhesives and grouts. Three hundred-odd categories in a mega-menu.
- Custom platform ("Powered by iSolution"), not WooCommerce or Shopify. Bootstrap, jQuery, a Swiper slider, Trustpilot, Klarna, Cookiebot, GA4.

## What makes their site easy, feature by feature

**Product page**

- A unit table before anything else: per m², single slab, pallet. Each row shows coverage and the was/now price. A customer can price a job in their head in five seconds.
- "Order by Pallet" or "Order by M2" as a radio. Pallet mode has two boxes, pallets and single slabs, with a "convert slabs into crates" helper. Area mode takes m² and works out the rest.
- The pallet line ("1 Pallet = 22 m²  £504.90") updates live under the inputs.
- Straight under the button: "Free delivery on full pallets to most postcodes. Split pallet orders will incur an £80 handling fee at checkout." Then "Delivery from Thursday 17th September." Then "This product is also available for collection, 3 locations" that expands to addresses, maps and "call to book collection".
- Klarna line: 20% deposit, six monthly instalments at 0% APR.
- Cut sample £2.49 button. A help box with phone, hours, email, WhatsApp.
- Tabs below: description, specification (tiles per m², thickness, slip grade, underfloor heating, a Declaration of Performance PDF), delivery and returns, and "Know before you order" (batches change; order 10 to 15% extra; a paid cutting service; coverage is at standard joint widths).
- "Finish the look" cross-sells edging, bullnose steps, setts and a levelling kit; "Installation materials" sells primer, grout and adhesive with coverage.
- Seventy-two reviews on the product, questions and answers with the store's replies, and a Trustpilot slider.
- Size variants as a dropdown (900 × 600 at 20 mm or 16 mm, 900 × 450).

**Tile calculator** (a modal on every product page)

- Width × length rows with a unit picker (m, mm, cm, inches, feet), "Add another wall/floor", 10% wastage added automatically and said so, total m², total price, add to cart from inside the modal.

**Category page**

- Filters by colour, suitability, finish, feature, size, shape and a per-m² price range; sort high/low; product count. Cards show the per-m² price with the was price struck, and "Sold out" plainly. A short paragraph of category copy at the foot for search engines.

**Site-wide**

- A scrolling announcement strip: "Quantity discounts: up to 10% off most indoor tiles, applied automatically at checkout."
- Header strip: phone numbers, hours, FREE Delivery*, Trustpilot, Open trade account. Search box in the header. A floating WhatsApp button.
- Home: slider, "Find your perfect look", showroom cards with hours and directions, category tiles, Trustpilot, trade account block, an FAQ accordion (samples, showrooms, delivery, trade, discounts, calculator).
- Delivery page: a full kerbside policy (pump truck, no grass or gravel, 18-tonne access, sign as damaged, redelivery charges), express (2–3 days, £20 a pallet), specified-day and Saturday options, AM/PM slots for a fee.
- Trade: a cash trade account, discounts on orders over £350, order by noon for two-day delivery, a dedicated team.
- No pop-up offer, no exit-intent, no discount pop-up. The only overlays are the cookie banner and the calculator. Their "offer" is the strip and the struck-through prices.

## What was left alone, and why

- **Showrooms, five of them.** The yard has one address. The demo makes that a strength ("a yard, not a website with a warehouse somewhere").
- **£80 split-pallet fee, £20 express, AM/PM slot fees.** Real numbers the yard has not given. Nothing is charged in the demo that the owner has not said.
- **A paid cutting service.** Royale says they offer one. The yard has not said so. It goes on the decisions list, not on the site.
- **Trustpilot and seventy-two reviews.** The demo shows the two reviews the store has. A review platform is on the yard list.
- **Size variants in a dropdown.** The yard sells each size as its own listing; the demo keeps that, with "pairs with" links between them.
- **Bathrooms, pergolas, decking.** Not what the yard sells.
- **Quantity discounts at checkout.** Not a yard policy yet; a trade account is.

## Where the yard can beat them

1. **Price, on every shared range**, once the owner confirms the price-match line.
2. **Distance.** Hemel to Watford is the same drive as Hemel to Mark Road, and Mark Road has the stone on the ground with no minimum to collect.
3. **The mixed pack sold honestly.** Royale's 19 m² pack is the same 16/16/16/12 composition as the yard's 18.19 m²; the demo shows the composition and the calculator never rounds it away.
4. **Real gardens.** Twenty-three customer photographs against Royale's renders. Royale's product photographs are strong, though; the half-day photo shoot on the yard list matters more after seeing theirs.
5. **The essentials cross-sell.** The warehouse database lists jointing compounds, primer, edging, steps, granite setts and cobbles that are not on the site at all. Royale sells all of these alongside the slab. Adding them is the biggest easy gain after go-live; it needs prices from the owner.
